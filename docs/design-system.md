# Design system

Material 3 Expressive is the design language. Benji Taylor's three principles decide how the product behaves. What the product looks like, and why, is in `docs/design-direction.md`: it chooses the values the tokens below carry. We build our own components on React Aria Components and style them only with tokens.

Why this and not a ready-made Material library is in `docs/decisions.md`, entries 4 to 6.

**Status, 2026-11-11.** What holds: rules 1 to 17, React Aria as the base of every component, the token pipeline, and the accessibility rules. What is being redesigned on card TS-030: the seed colour, the typeface, how a page looks as a whole, and the home page. Until TS-030 merges, the values in "Tokens" below are the ones in the code. The decision that replaces entries 4 and 16 arrives with that card.

## Principles

From _Family Values_ (benji.org/family-values): "Simplicity ensures accessibility, fluidity maintains continuity of experience, and delight fosters meaningful connections."

| Principle  | What it means here                                                                      |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Simplicity | The fundamentals are at hand. Everything else appears when it becomes relevant.         |
| Fluidity   | Every change of view is a movement the person can follow. "We fly instead of teleport." |
| Delight    | Selective emphasis: the same polish everywhere, with special moments placed on purpose. |

## Rules

Each rule can be checked in review or by a test.

### Simplicity

1. A view or sheet has one primary action.
2. A task that is brief or belongs to the current view opens in a sheet over that view: confirming, quick editing, warnings. A new page is for work that stands on its own.
3. A sheet holds one piece of content or one action. A sequence of sheets shows a title and a back control.
4. Settings a person rarely needs sit behind a clearly named control, never on the main view.

"Sheet" in these rules means either overlay. Use the Dialog component for a confirmation or a warning, and the Sheet component for quick editing.

### Fluidity

5. Route changes animate in the direction of travel: forward and to the right move one way, back and to the left the other.
6. An element that is on screen before and after a change stays the same element. It moves; it is never removed and redrawn.
7. A label or number that changes animates the change: timer digits, totals, a button going from "Continue" to "Confirm".
8. Every animation can be interrupted by the next input.
9. With reduced motion requested, movement and scaling are replaced by a fade or nothing.

### Delight

10. Every screen gets the same polish, including the ones used rarely: settings, empty states, errors.
11. The strongest moments go to events that are significant and rare: a first organisation, the first tracked hour, closing a week.
12. Actions repeated many times a day, such as starting and stopping a timer, get subtle feedback that never delays the next action.

### Material and accessibility

13. Components read colour, type, shape, spacing, elevation and motion from tokens. Raw colours and sizes fail `pnpm check:tokens`.
14. Light and dark schemes come from one seed colour. Text meets 4.5:1 contrast in both.
15. Everything works by keyboard, with a visible focus indicator.
16. Pages meet WCAG 2.2 AA; automated checks run in the pipeline and a manual keyboard pass is part of TS-023.
17. Layout follows Material's window sizes: the navigation is a bar along the bottom when the window is under 600px wide, and a column beside the page from 600px.

## From idea to shipped screen

The steps a screen goes through. They are step 3 of "Flow of one card" in `docs/ways-of-working.md`.

1. **The card names the task.** What the person came to do, and the one primary action of the screen (rule 1).
2. **The owner approves the look first.** The card names its reference: the visual direction, and the nearest screen already built or a sketch. The owner approves it before the card enters Ready.
3. **The layout is settled before code.** At 390px and at 1280px, in the light and the dark scheme, with the empty state and a full one.
4. **Missing components come first.** Each goes through "Adding a component" below.
5. **The page is built.** A page in the shell follows "Shell"; a page outside it follows "Pages outside the shell".
6. **The rules are checked.** Rules 1 to 9 and 13 to 17 by a test or in review. Rules 10 to 12 are matters of judgement: the owner checks them in step 7.
7. **The owner accepts the screenshots.** At both widths in both schemes, in the pull request. For a signed-in page they come from the browser tests.
8. **Merge.**

### Colour schemes

**Light is the default.** The server renders `data-color-scheme="light"` on `html` for a person who has chosen nothing, whatever their device prefers: the owner looked at both schemes and chose light as the product's face (`docs/design-direction.md`, "Light is the product's face"). A person who chooses Dark in Settings gets `data-color-scheme="dark"`. Only a person who chooses "Match my device" gets a page with no attribute, and there `:root`'s own `color-scheme: light dark` lets each role follow the device setting.

`data-color-scheme="light"` or `data-color-scheme="dark"` forces one scheme: on the `html` element for the whole page, or on any element for that part of it. Because every role already carries both values, components need no `dark:` variants. An element that forces a scheme must also set its own background and text colour (`bg-surface text-on-surface`), since those are inherited as colours already worked out. The `theme-color` the browser's own frame takes is named once, for the scheme the page is held to, and once for each scheme where the page follows the device (`app/root.tsx`).

### The spring

The spring is defined once, by its stiffness and damping ratio. `pnpm tokens` derives two forms from those numbers:

- `--md-sys-motion-spring-stiffness` and `--md-sys-motion-spring-damping`, the values the Motion library would take (mass 1);
- `--md-sys-motion-spring-easing`, a CSS `linear()` curve sampled from the same spring, with `--md-sys-motion-spring-duration`, the time it takes to settle within 0.1% of its target.

### Changing a token

To change the seed colour, edit the one `SEED_COLOUR` line in `scripts/tokens.ts`, run `pnpm tokens`, then `pnpm test`, and commit the script, `app/styles/tokens.css` and `app/styles/theme-colour.ts` together; then run `pnpm icons` and `node scripts/architecture-figure.ts`, which draw from the same values. The contrast test checks every text role against its background in both schemes; if a pair falls below 4.5:1, raise the scheme's contrast level in `scripts/tokens.ts` and leave the test alone. The type scale, corner scale, spacing unit, elevation, durations, easings and the spring change the same way. To change a typeface, put its files in `app/fonts/` with their licence, declare them in `app/styles/fonts.css` and edit `TYPEFACES`.

### What `pnpm check:tokens` rejects

Under `app/`, outside the generated `app/styles/tokens.css`:

- a hex colour, such as `#fff` or `#4f5bd5`;
- a utility with a bracketed value, such as `w-[13px]`, `bg-[#fff]`, `text-[color:red]`, `bg-primary/[0.5]` or `min-[320px]:`;
- a bracketed declaration used as a class, such as `[mask-type:luminance]`;
- a style read from a sibling's attribute, such as `peer-data-focused:` or `peer-aria-invalid:` ("State from another element", below).

Bracketed variants that only select a state, such as `data-[state=open]:`, are allowed. The check reads text, not syntax: it also reports a hex colour in a comment, and three or four hex digits after a bare `#` (write "issue 123", not `#123`).

The specification column names the token files read on 11 Nov 2026 in Material's published token set, `tokens/versions/latest/sass/_<name>.scss` in github.com/material-components/material-web ("Google Material 3, Version 34.0.21"). Widths, paddings and the 48px touch target that the token files leave out come from the Compose Material 3 sources (github.com/androidx/androidx, `compose/material3`). m3.material.io renders its specification pages with script, so no value was taken from it.

Where a component departs from those files:

- **Corners are tighter than Material's.** The corner scale keeps Material's step names at smaller values (`docs/design-direction.md`, "Shape and space"), so a button has 10px corners and is not a pill, and a dialog's corner is 12px. Each component still takes the step Material gives it.
- **A selected switch keeps its handle's colour** under the pointer and in focus. Material turns it to `primary-container`, which is the colour of the track in the dark scheme.
- **A hovered field darkens its label** to `on-surface` (`on-error-container` when invalid). Material keeps `on-surface-variant`, which measures 4.33:1 on the hover layer in the light scheme, under the 4.5:1 that rule 14 requires.
- **A destructive confirmation uses `danger`.** The button that confirms a deletion or anything else that cannot be undone is `variant="danger"`, the error colour; the button that opens that confirmation is `outlined-danger`. Where the confirmation asks for a name to be typed, as deleting an organisation does, it stays disabled until the name is typed exactly (`FormSheet`'s `submitVariant` and `isSubmitDisabled`).
- **A row with two things to do has no menu.** On Projects and Clients the record's name is a button that opens its sheet, where it is edited, and the sheet holds Archive or Restore (`docs/design-direction.md`, rule 2).
- **A sheet is a dialog from 840px.** Along the bottom of a wide window it reads as a stray strip, so from the `expanded` breakpoint it sits in the middle, 480px at most, with a margin on every side (taste HT-C).
- **The sheet has no drag handle**, because it is not dragged. It closes by Escape, by pressing outside, or by its own buttons.
- **A snackbar's text is a `status`, not an `alert`**, so it is announced when the screen reader is next idle. React Aria's toast defaults to `alert`.

### State from another element

A component often styles one element from the state of another: a field's label from its input. Where the state is read from depends on where the other element is.

- **The element itself or an ancestor:** React Aria's attribute, directly (`data-focused:`, `group-data-focused:`).
- **A sibling:** through the container the two share. The container is a named group and the styled element asks whether the group holds the state: `group/field` on the container, `group-has-data-focused/field:` on the label. `app/components/field.ts` does this for a field's label and line.
- **A sibling's own pseudo-class** is safe too: `peer-placeholder-shown:` is how a label knows its input is empty.
- **Never a sibling's attribute:** `peer-data-…:` and `peer-aria-…:` fail `pnpm check:tokens`.

The reason is a defect in WebKit 27.2, which is Safari's engine, found on 2026-11-11. When a page's stylesheet has any rule for a pseudo-element inside a form control (`::placeholder`, `::file-selector-button`; Tailwind's reset has both), WebKit does not restyle the siblings of an `input` or a `textarea` for an attribute written on it during an event. React Aria writes `data-focused` and `data-hovered` during events. The label of a text field stayed on the line a person was typing on; Chromium was right, so no test saw it. The element's own attributes, an ancestor's, `:has()` and pseudo-classes are all followed correctly.

The smallest page that shows it. The text turns red on focus in Chromium and stays black in WebKit 27.2:

```html
<style>
  ::placeholder { opacity: 1 }
  input[data-on] ~ span { color: red }
</style>
<input><span>red once the input has focus</span>
<script>
  const input = document.querySelector("input");
  input.addEventListener("focus", () => input.setAttribute("data-on", ""));
</script>
```

`e2e/field-label.spec.ts` holds the fields to the right behaviour in both engines, and the component tests as a whole run in WebKit as well as Chromium (`playwright.config.ts`).

### How the components move

- **Overlays enter and leave with movement.** A menu and a select's list grow out of their button; a dialog rises and grows; a sheet slides up from the bottom edge (from 840px it fades in growing from 95%); a snackbar rises; a tooltip grows. The spring (`ease-spring duration-spring`) carries what arrives; leaving is short and accelerates away.
- **A change of selection moves the thing that marks it.** The tab indicator slides to the selected tab, the switch handle travels along its track, and a field's label floats up when the field takes focus.
- **Everything is a CSS transition that reads the motion tokens**, so the next input turns a movement around from where it is (rule 8) and nothing waits for an animation to end (rule 12). The Motion library is not installed yet: none of these components needs what a transition cannot do. It joins with the first component that does, such as a gesture.
- **With reduced motion requested, only opacity and colour change.** Classes that move or scale sit under `motion-safe:`, and each moving element has a `motion-reduce:` class that narrows what it transitions. `e2e/reduced-motion.spec.ts` operates every component and fails if anything animates position or size.

### The gallery

`app/routes/design.tsx` is the `/design` route. It is registered by `app/routes.ts` only under `pnpm dev`, and in a build made with `DESIGN_ROUTE=1`, which is the build `pnpm test:e2e` makes and then deletes. A normal `pnpm build` leaves it out: production answers 404 (`test/design-route.test.ts`).

- Each half sets `data-color-scheme`, and overlays opened from a half render inside it, so they take its scheme.
- Hover, focus and pressed are held in place by setting the attributes React Aria sets for them.
- An overlay opens from its button. `/design?open=dialog&scheme=dark` opens one as the page loads; `open` is `select`, `menu`, `dialog`, `sheet`, `tooltip` or `snackbar`.
- `?open=wait` holds the WaitClock row waiting as the page loads; otherwise its switch does. Its "Send invoice" waits three seconds when pressed.
- `?tab=`, `?period=`, `?q=` and `?status=` drive the LinkTabs, PeriodStepper and FilterBar examples.

### Adding a component

1. Read Material's token file for it and note the values: sizes, shape, colour roles for each state.
2. Render the React Aria component you mean to build on with `renderToString` and look for `style=`. If there is one, build on the hooks instead (`docs/security.md`, "What components do to live under the policy").
3. Write `app/components/<Name>.tsx`: one exported component with a small prop surface, each prop documented. Take colour, type, shape, spacing, elevation and motion from token utilities; use the shared pieces above for state, focus and motion. If it moves, give it a `motion-reduce:` class.
4. Add it to `app/routes/design.tsx` in every state it has.
5. Add a test to `e2e/components.spec.ts` that finds it by role and accessible name and operates it by keyboard alone. If it opens, add it to the list in `e2e/accessibility.spec.ts`; if it moves, operate it in `e2e/reduced-motion.spec.ts`.
6. Add a row to the table above.
7. Run the checks in the Definition of Done (`docs/ways-of-working.md`).

`app/shell/destinations.ts` is the one list: Timesheet and Time off under Track; Projects, Clients and Team under Organize; Approvals and Reports under Review; Workspace settings under no heading, in that order (`docs/navigation.md`). Nothing else writes one of these addresses. To add a destination, add it to the list and add `app/routes/app/<id>.tsx` with a `PageHeading`. The navigation starts with the place for the timer's control (`app/shell/NavigationTimer.tsx`, TS-051), which draws nothing until it is built. In the bar the headings are left out and the labels are `label-medium`, one line each and each as wide as its words, so the eight an owner is shown fit in 390px, with the number on Approvals. ### Pages inside a destination

A page below a destination belongs to it: the navigation marks that destination as current, and a move to the page from another destination goes forward or back by the destination's place in the list. `destinationIndex` in `app/shell/destinations.ts` decides it from the address: a path that continues a destination's address with a `/` belongs to that destination. The members and invitations under Team are such pages. Timesheet's address is the organisation's own, the start of every other, so a page below Timesheet cannot be found that way and must be named in `destinationIndex`. The week view (TS-013) will be the first.

A move between two pages of one destination starts no transition today: `useTravel` returns `null` when the destination does not change. A card that wants movement there, such as TS-013 for the previous and the next day, adds it and says which way is forward.

### Pages outside the shell

Three kinds exist: the home page (`app/routes/home.tsx`), the two sign-in pages, and an invitation's page (`app/routes/invitations.$id.tsx`). The last two share the frame in `app/routes/auth/AuthPage.tsx`. Each has one `main` landmark and one `h1`, and takes every value from tokens like any other page. They have no navigation.

Four constraints apply to every page, and bite hardest on a public page with pictures and its own type. The first three are in `docs/security.md`, "Content Security Policy":

- `img-src 'self' data:`: an image is a file of our own, or inline data. None is loaded from another host.
- `font-src 'self'`: a font is a file of our own.
- No `style` attribute in the HTML the server sends: the browser ignores it. Style with classes.
- `pnpm check:tokens` refuses a hex colour and a bracketed Tailwind value under `app/`.

### The 600px rule

One `nav` element with one list of links is drawn two ways, by CSS alone (rule 17). Tailwind's `medium:` variant is Material's medium window size, 600px (`--breakpoint-medium` in `app/app.css`); `max-medium:` is everything narrower.

| Window      | Navigation | Where                                                  | Drawn                       |
| ----------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Under 600px | Bar        | Along the bottom edge, 64px tall, under a 2px ink rule | The links side by side      |
| From 600px  | Column     | Beside the page, under the band, 96px wide             | The links one under another |

An item is its label, at least 48px tall: `label-large` in the column, `label-medium` in the bar so that every destination an owner is shown fits; the column also shows the headings Track, Organize and Review above their links. The active destination has `aria-current="page"` and a 2px ink rule, under its label in the bar and before it in the column; nothing is filled. No script measures the window: the server sends the same HTML to every width, and the navigation works with script off.

### How a change of destination moves

React's `<ViewTransition>` (in the installed React, 19.3.1) starts the browser's view transition when React Router commits a navigation; the animations are CSS in `app/app.css` and read the motion tokens. There is no `style` attribute in the HTML: React names the parts through the CSSOM when a transition starts.

- **Direction (rule 5).** A destination later in the list is forward of an earlier one. `useTravel` in `app/shell/transitions.ts` compares the destination arriving with the one on screen and gives the content the class `shell-forward` or `shell-back`. Going forward the old page leaves towards the start and the new one arrives from the end; going back is the mirror image. It works the same for a press on a destination, a link inside a page and the browser's Back button, because it reads only where the page was and where it is going.
- **Axis.** Under 600px the destinations lie side by side, so the content moves across. From 600px they are stacked, so it moves up and down. The distance is eight spacing units.
- **What moves (rule 6).** Only the content. The active indicator is one element with one view-transition name: it travels from the old destination to the new one on the spring. The indicator is the 2px ink rule under or beside the current label.
- **The navigation is not in the transition.** The browser does not deliver a press to an element that is part of a running view transition, so the navigation stays an ordinary part of the page and can be pressed at any moment.
- **The document is not in the transition either.** When the content's `<ViewTransition>` changes size, as it does whenever the new page is taller or shorter than the old one, React counts the whole document as changed and captures it (`::view-transition-old(root)` and `-new(root)`). While the document is captured Chromium hit-tests every press to `<html>`, so nothing on the page, the navigation included, can be pressed until the transition has played. The content's boundary therefore sits inside a second `<ViewTransition default="none">` in `app/routes/app/layout.tsx`, which never animates and takes the change of size, so React leaves the document out of the transition whatever the pages' heights.
- **Interruption (rule 8).** React holds the next transition until the running one has finished. A press on a destination and the browser's Back and Forward therefore end the running transition first (`finishRunningTransition`, through `document.activeViewTransition`): its animations jump to their end and the new page arrives at once. In a browser without that property the next transition follows the running one, a third of a second later at most. Each skip rejects the skipped transition's `ready` promise; the shell's own skip catches it, and the browser's skip of a transition React supersedes is kept from being reported as an uncaught error by one `unhandledrejection` listener that the root installs (`ignoreSkippedTransitions`), which lets every other rejection through. A press this early often comes while the page that just arrived is still revalidating after a fetcher's action (the timer saves the time zone as a new organisation's Timesheet arrives). React Router 8.5.1 lets that revalidation end a navigation that is still discovering its route, with the old page's routes and data: the address changes and the page does not. So every page arrives knowing every route (`routeDiscovery: { mode: "initial" }` in `react-router.config.ts`) and a navigation has nothing to discover.
- **Only a change of destination starts a transition.** The content's `<ViewTransition>` has the class `none` in every other render. Without that, React starts a transition for any render that reaches it, and each one holds back the next navigation.
- **Reduced motion (rule 9).** Nothing moves or changes size: each part takes its new place at once and the content cross-fades.
- **The two views of the sign-in page** move the same way, with the same classes (`AuthPage` in `app/routes/auth/AuthPage.tsx`), except with reduced motion requested: there a change of view is not a view transition at all (`changeStep`). The new view is in its place at once and fades in with a CSS transition of opacity. A view transition whose parts only fade is not enough: when the new view measures the same as the old one, React 19.3.1 leaves the rest of the page out of the transition, and to do so it starts an animation from script that holds the `width` and `height` of `::view-transition` at nothing. A style sheet cannot stop an animation that script starts, and whether two views measure the same depends on how the person's address wraps in the sentence around it. `e2e/auth-accessibility.spec.ts`, "with reduced motion requested", counts the view transitions the page starts and expects none. The band at the top, the photograph and the foot are outside the transition and hold still.
- **A browser without view transitions** navigates with no animation.

### Focus and landmarks

- The first Tab stop is "Skip to main content", off the top edge until it has the focus. It moves the focus to the `main` element.
- Landmarks: one `banner` (the band), one `navigation` named "Main" and one `main`.
- After a move between pages the focus is on the new page's `h1`, and the title has changed. The same happens on arriving in the shell by a link from a page outside it.
- A page the browser loads whole leaves the focus alone.

### The content column

Every page of an organisation is drawn in one column, at most 800px wide (`max-w-200`, `INSIDE_COLUMN` in `app/routes/app/layout.tsx`), beside the navigation. Headings, tabs, forms, rules, sheets and the week's grid all stop at its right edge (audit W22). A page does not set its own maximum width for a block; a narrower block (text at 640px, a form at 480px) sets its own. A grid wider than the column on a narrow window scrolls inside its own frame.

### While the page waits

While a move to another page, or a form sent by a fetcher, waits for the server, a bar 3px tall runs across the top edge of the window, above the band (`app/shell/PendingBar.tsx`, drawn once by the root layout, so every page has it; audit W09). It appears only once the wait has lasted 150ms, so a quick answer shows nothing. A segment two fifths of the width runs across it in the band's ink (`on-sheet`), for as long as the wait lasts; with reduced motion requested the bar stands still across the whole width. While it is shown it is a `progressbar` named "Loading", with no value. The `main` element is `aria-busy` for the whole wait, from the first moment. `e2e/shell-pending.spec.ts` holds this.

A move to **another destination** that has waited 150ms does more (TS-096, `app/shell/arrival.ts`): the person has their answer that the press landed. The active indicator travels to the destination, and the page they left gives way to the destination's shape (`DestinationSkeleton.tsx`): its heading, the sentence under it and the body its page has (a day, a table, a list, a form or a board of cards), in `Skeleton` blocks the size of what they stand for, breathing slowly. The shape arrives the way the page would have, sliding in the direction of travel. The page left stays mounted, hidden, under React's `<Activity>`. When the page arrives it takes the shape's place and its sections come in one after another (`data-reveal` on `main`): each rises two spacing units and fades in, 40ms after the one before. A second press during the wait swaps the shape for the next destination's at once, and the indicator jumps with it; the page left never comes back in between (rule 8). The swap is drawn from a layout effect, outside the navigation's transition: a view transition started for it would hold the page's own arrival back until it had played. Each section's reveal fills backwards only, so it lets go of the section when it ends and leaves no stacking context behind. A move counts as late by itself, not by its location's key, since Back and Forward return to an entry with the key it had. A move within a destination (a filter, another week, one project's own page) and a sent form keep the page on screen with the bar alone: the person is still looking at it. With reduced motion requested the shape stands still and the sections fade in together. A screen reader hears "Loading" and the destination's name once, from a status line; the shapes are hidden from it. `e2e/shell-loading.spec.ts` holds this.

The page never waits for its shape either: the moment the router has the page's data, before React draws it, a view transition still playing (the shape's slide) ends at once (`app/shell/arrival.ts`), since React holds the next page until a running transition has finished.

The Timesheet keeps the same conventions inside its pages (TS-096). Track time holds the shape of its fields (`Skeleton`, a `status` named "Loading your projects") while it reads the day's rules, never a sentence that the page is reading. The Day view's sections, which move as one block between days, are marked `data-reveal-sections`, so on arrival they come in one after another after the block's place; with reduced motion they stand still. A timer's start or stop is a quick action: from the navigation, the Day view's Start again or Stop timer, or Track time's Start timer (the sheet closes at once), every timer on the page draws it as done while it is on its way (`usePendingTimer`, `app/timesheet/pending.ts`, read from the requests themselves). A refusal leaves the page's data as it was, so the timers go back by themselves, and the form that sent it says why: beside itself, or in the snackbar when the sheet has closed. `e2e/timesheet-motion.spec.ts` holds this.

A wait that is not a page has the WaitClock (`app/components/WaitClock.tsx`) instead, in the control that started it: sending and checking a sign-in code, sending an invoice or an estimate, an export, a PDF or a ZIP, a CSV import's upload, preview and import, a webhook's test, and every other form that keeps the person on the page while the server works. A page load never has it: the page's skeleton and the bar above stand for that (TS-096).

- **Nothing shows for 400ms** (`medium4`). Then, by opacity alone over 200ms, the control's words at rest give way to the clock and what it is doing: "Sending…", "Checking…", "Exporting…". A quick answer shows nothing, so nothing flashes.
- **The control keeps its size.** Its words at rest and its words while waiting stand in one grid cell (`app/components/WaitingLabel.tsx`), so it is as wide as the wider of the two from the start, and as wide while it waits. The words while waiting are the verb of the label as something being done (`app/components/doing.ts`: "Save" says "Saving…", "Send estimate" says "Sending…"), or `pendingLabel`.
- **A pending control sends nothing more.** A Button is `isPending` (React Aria's): it cannot be pressed, its form cannot be submitted with Enter, and it stays in the focus order, so the focus is not thrown off the button just pressed. Its name stays its words at rest, and React Aria announces it as pending. On a page's own `<Form>` the pressed button is pending and the others are disabled until the answer has loaded (`useSubmitting`, `app/shell/submitting.ts`); a fetcher's form is pending while its fetcher is not idle (`ActionForm`, `FormSheet`, `SecretFormSheet`); the sign-in pages read `running` from `useOneAtATime`.
- **A download is a DownloadLink.** It fetches the file and saves it under the name `Content-Disposition` gives, with the clock in the link until the file has arrived; an answer that is not the file opens as a page, so a refusal is seen. Without script it is an ordinary link with `download`.
- **The hand turns by `transform` alone,** one turn every 1.2 seconds on the standard easing, so it sweeps and slows into twelve; the compositor draws it while the page is busy with the answer. With reduced motion it stands at twelve, and the clock and its words still appear.
- **On amber or ink it is one colour.** On a filled or tonal button the whole clock is drawn in the button's text colour (`tone="one"`); elsewhere the ring is ink (`on-surface`) and the hand amber (`primary-container`).

`e2e/wait-clock.spec.ts` holds the network for two seconds and checks Continue, Create organisation and an export: the clock is not seen before 400ms and is seen soon after, the control keeps its size, a second press or Enter sends nothing, and the export still downloads without script. It checks the hand's turn and its stillness under reduced motion, and scans the waiting row with axe at 390px and 1280px in both schemes.

### How a page loads

A load and a reload draw the page the server sent, in its final place, and nothing on it moves afterwards (TS-096). What that takes, each item a cause of the "blink on refresh" it removed:

- **A time is simply there.** `Digits` used to make every character of every time rise and fade in whenever it was drawn, so on each load and each arrival every figure on the page vanished and came back. Only a character that replaces another while the time is looked at moves now (`tick-in`).
- **Text is set in its final width from the first frame.** Each face has a metric-matched stand-in (`Sofia Sans Fallback`, `Sofia Sans Condensed Fallback` in `app/styles/fonts.css`): Arial, Helvetica or Roboto resized with `size-adjust`, `ascent-override` and `descent-override` to Sofia Sans's widths and line box, next in the family after the face. The two faces every page uses are preloaded with the stylesheet.
- **The built files are kept.** Everything under `/assets/` is named by its content and served `immutable` for a year (`public/_headers`), so a reload asks the server for the page alone; before, it revalidated about 60 files and the stylesheet's round trip held back the first paint.
- **The page is answered near its database.** The Worker runs beside D1 (`placement` in `wrangler.jsonc`, docs/decisions.md 34), so the server's answer, which a reload waits for with the old page still on screen, is no longer a dozen crossings of the Atlantic.
- **The scheme is the server's.** The page is rendered in the person's scheme from the cookie, so its first frame is in it; no script changes it after.
- **No whole-page view transition.** `@view-transition { navigation: auto }` was tried and left out: Chromium abandons it when the sign-in page's form, sent as a whole page, starts its own transition on the way out, and reports that as an uncaught `InvalidStateError` the page cannot catch. A reload is not a navigation browsers animate in any case; it draws the same page again in the same place, which is what the items above make true.

### Hover, press and focus

Every control shows its state with Material's state layer on the `transition-control` curve (colour 200ms, background 150ms, the pressed shape on the spring). A link's hover is a change of its underline: a link in text thickens it, a link standing alone in a bar (the destinations, the band) draws it in, both over 150ms (`transition-link`, `hover-underline`). The focus indicator is the same 2px ring everywhere and never animates.

### What a solid page learns from Royal London

Studied for TS-096 (`timesifier/research/royal-london-motion.md`, beside the repository, with the frame strips and the numbers). Royal London has no cross-document view transition, no prefetch, no skeletons and no staggered reveal, and still feels solid, for three reasons: the browser holds the old page until the new one can paint, the new page's first paint is the header, identical and in the same place, and every picture lands in a box that was already its size and tinted. Their only layout shift (CLS 0.033 at 1280px, 0.037 at 390px) is the brand face swapping in over a fallback of other metrics. State changes are colour and border transitions of 150 to 250ms; a link's underline draws in 600ms on hover; their own animations ignore reduced motion. Timesifier keeps what works (a frame that never repaints, space held before data, short colour transitions) and does what they do not: metric-matched fallback faces, prefetch on intent, one cross-document transition that matches the in-app one, and reduced motion everywhere.

### When a page cannot be shown

An error page says what happened, by the status, in the words of `app/shell/error-words.ts`: 404 "There's no page here"; 400 "That address asks for something that isn't valid", with the sentence the loader threw if it gave one; 403 "This page isn't yours to open"; anything else from the server, and any failure, "Something went wrong on our side". A request that was the person's own mistake is never said to be the server's (audit W14).

- **Inside an organisation that is the person's,** the page is drawn where the page would have been, with the band, the organisation's name and the navigation kept, and a link to the Timesheet (`ErrorBoundary` in `app/routes/app/organisation.tsx`). That covers an address that asks for something not valid (`?view=bogus`, `/approvals?status=bogus`), a page that failed, and any address under the organisation that holds no page: `app/routes/app/not-found.tsx` answers those (`*` in `app/routes.ts`) with 404.
- **Everywhere else** the root's error page is drawn in the sign-in page's frame, with the band's lockup alone and one link: to `/app` under `/app`, to the home page outside it. That covers an address no route answers and an organisation that is nobody's or not the person's. A destination the person's role is not shown is answered the same way, on purpose: it is the same 404 a stranger gets (`docs/security.md`, "Organisations and roles").
- **The scheme is kept.** The root's error page is often drawn before any loader ran, so it has no answer from the root loader to read the scheme from. The server entry then reads the cookie itself and provides it (`app/shell/request-scheme.ts`), and in the browser the page keeps the scheme it was drawn in. A person who chose dark is never shown a white error page. `test/pages/errors.test.ts` and `e2e/shell-errors.spec.ts` hold all of this.

### Choosing a colour scheme

Settings offers Light, Dark and "Match my device" as one radio group, Light first because it is what a person who has chosen nothing has. Each choice is stored in the cookie `__Host-color-scheme` as `light`, `dark` or `system` (`docs/security.md`); with no cookie the page is light. The root loader reads the cookie and the server renders `data-color-scheme` on `html`, or leaves it off for `system`, so a page arrives in the chosen scheme with no flash and no inline script. With script the choice is saved as it is made and the page changes without loading again; without script the form has a Save button and the page loads again.

### Locale

The root loader chooses a locale from `Accept-Language` (`app/shell/locale.ts`; `en-GB` when the request names none the server can format) and `app/root.tsx` gives it to React Aria's `I18nProvider`, which its controls read (a number field's separators, a date picker's order). The interface text is English and `<html lang="en">` does not change.

The dates and times a page writes do not follow that locale: see "Dates and times" below.

### Dates and times

Every page writes a date, a period, a time and a length of time through `app/time/format.ts`, one form for each kind (audit W12). The Timesheet's hooks (`app/timesheet/format.ts`), Reports' words (`app/reports/format.ts`), Team's and Approvals' (`app/teams/words.ts`) and Time off's (`app/leave/words.ts`) call it; nothing else in `app/` makes an `Intl.DateTimeFormat` for visible text.

| Kind                              | Function                            | Written                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A day                             | `formatDay(day)`                    | Wednesday 11 November                                                                                           |
| A day in a week's row of seven    | `formatDay(day, "short")`           | Wed 11                                                                                                          |
| A day that may be in another year | `formatDayWithYear(day)`            | Wednesday 11 November 2026                                                                                      |
| A period                          | `formatRange(first, last)`          | 9–15 November 2026; 30 November – 6 December 2026; 28 December 2026 – 3 January 2027; one day, 11 November 2026 |
| A month                           | `formatMonth(day)`                  | November 2026                                                                                                   |
| A time of day                     | `formatTime(instant, zone)`         | 09:30, on the 24-hour clock; midnight is 00:00                                                                  |
| A stretch of time                 | `formatTimeRange(start, end, zone)` | 21:33–02:33; while it runs, 21:33–now                                                                           |
| A moment something was done       | `formatDayAndTime(instant, zone)`   | Wednesday 11 November at 09:30                                                                                  |
| A length of time                  | `formatDuration(seconds)`           | 5:00, 0:07: hours and whole minutes                                                                             |

The dash between two numbers is closed up; between two dates with words in them it is spaced. A page puts a period after its own words: "the week of 9–15 November 2026".

The text depends on nothing about where it is written. The locale is fixed (`en-GB`), every instant is read in a zone the caller names (the person's or the organisation's, decision 26), and a day, which has no time, is read as UTC. Intl is asked only for the names of days and months, through `formatToParts`, and the module puts the parts in their order itself: V8 writes `{ weekday: "short", day: "numeric" }` as "9 Mon" and Safari's engine as "Mon 9", and the difference made every Timesheet in Safari fail to hydrate (React error #418, audit W06). `test/time/format.test.ts` holds every form; `test/node/date-parity.test.ts` writes them on machines with other locales and zones and from an engine that orders the parts the other way, and renders the Timesheet's week of days on each; the gallery's Dates section is checked in Chromium and WebKit (`e2e/design.spec.ts`), and `e2e/dates.spec.ts` loads the Timesheet in a US-English browser in California.

A right-to-left language is passed over for the next one in the header. React Aria takes the direction of its arrow keys from the locale, and the interface is not mirrored; when a translated, mirrored interface arrives, this rule goes and `html` gets its `dir`.
